📐 The Scoring Model (0-100)

Total score (0–100) = Outcome (30) + Market validation (25) + Experience (20) + Pathway quality (15) + Pipeline progression (10) Outcome (30 pts) = 30 × clamp( (shrunk_rate − 0.75) / 0.25, 0, 1 ) where shrunk_rate = (approvals + k·p₀) / (decisions + k), k = 3, p₀ = 0.906 p₀ is VERIFIED population base rate for filed NDA/BLA (BIO / Biomedtracker / Informa, 2011–2020, n = 1,453) Market validation (25 pts) = 25 × [ 0.6 × share of priced approval events with positive T+1 move + 0.4 × clamp(median T+1 move / +25%, 0, 1) ] Experience (20 pts) = 20 × clamp( log2(1 + decisions tracked) / log2(1 + 12), 0, 1 ) Pathway quality (15 pts) = 15 × [ share Priority Review − 0.5 × share Accelerated Approval ] (clamped 0…1) Pipeline progression (10) = 10 × advanced_to_next_phase / (advanced + paused/clinical-hold) — only where pipeline scorecard is verified deep dive; otherwise 10 pts redistributed pro-rata and company labelled lower confidence.
  • Shrinkage toward verified external base rate, not zero. Outcome used to be Wilson 95% lower bound. That punished thin evidence asymmetrically: 1 approval/0 CRLs scored ~21%, implying 79% failure rate that no published dataset supports — FDA approves ~90.6% of filed applications. Now shrinks toward verified prior with k=3 pseudo-observations, so one approval scores 92.9% and company only departs from base rate once enough verified decisions justify it.
  • Calibration band keeps component discriminating. Because shrinkage compresses toward 90.6%, shrunk rate mapped through 75%–100% band anchored on published spread (BIO per-disease-area filing-to-approval 82.5% cardiovascular to 100% allergy). Population base rate earns 0.62 of 30 points — average filer, not top score — while sponsor with CRL falls sharply. Raw rate and Wilson lower bound still published as statistics.
  • Market validation and Experience re-weighted upward (20→25 and 15→20): market term only component built on exchange data, longer verified record main protection against reading luck as skill.
  • Accelerated approval treated as risk factor (−0.5 weight) because granted on surrogate endpoint that confirmatory trial must later verify; several accelerated approvals in dataset later withdrawn (Ukoniq, Relyvrio, Pepaxto, Exkivity).
  • Confidence reported alongside every score: High = ≥8 verified decisions and deep-dive pipeline; Moderate = ≥3 decisions; Low = fewer, or FDA-decisions-only view. Scores never presented without confidence tier.
  • Grades: A ≥80, B 65–79, C 50–64, D 35–49, E <35.

Regenerated by scripts/build_company_scores.py from fda_decisions_master.csv, fda_crl_master.csv, stock_price_snapshots.csv and company_scorecards.csv. No number typed by hand.

… CRL rows: the 58 deep-verified rows (news-corroborated, stock-reaction notes where available) plus 400 rows added 2026-09-17 from FDA's own CRL transparency database — every FDA-published CRL 2002–2026 naming a letter date and application number, each row with two official source links (openFDA CRL API query + Drugs@FDA application page). Irregularities worth tracking, including repeat-CRL cases. C004 (Outlook Therapeutics / Lytenava) marked RESOLVED: after third CRL on 2025-12-31 BLA resubmitted and approved on 2026-07-24.

CRL = Complete Response Letter — FDA's formal rejection stating application cannot be approved in present form. Requires sponsor to address deficiencies and resubmit. Track record of CRLs is critical for decision engine — repeat CRLs (e.g. Aldeyra/Reproxalap: 3 CRLs same indication) are strong negative signal. How tickers were assigned to the 400 new rows: only a repository-verified name match or an exact SEC-registrant title match (legal suffixes removed) was accepted; the trial of a looser matcher produced 7 provably wrong matches (e.g. biotech "Swedish Orphan Biovitrum" → wave-energy company Eco Wave Power) and was rejected. … new rows therefore carry a blank ticker and an explicit unresolved-sponsor flag; drug/indication text stays "See FDA letter" because FDA's database has no structured field for them.

🔍 Verification Audit — Every Master Row Re-checked Against Its Primary Source

A source link is only a claim that a row matches its source. This audit actually opens the primary openFDA Drugs@FDA record for each of the 1,427 novel-approval rows and compares the sponsor, brand name and decision date field by field, so a wrong date or a swapped company is caught mechanically rather than by eye.

What the statuses mean. MATCH — date, brand and sponsor all agree with openFDA. MATCH_DATE_ONLY — date and brand agree, sponsor wording differs (ownership change or legal-entity naming). MATCH_VIA_GENERIC — openFDA publishes current labelling, so a discontinued or genericised product shows its generic/successor name; the generic reconciles. DATE_DIFFERS_FROM_DRUGSFDA_1_3D — FDA's published NME approval report and Drugs@FDA differ by 1–3 days on the same approval; both sources are official, this is a known reporting difference, not an error. NO_APPL_NUMBER — the row cites no Drugs@FDA application number, so it cannot be machine-cross-checked here (it still carries its FDA source links). NOT_IN_OPENFDA — the application is cited but openFDA returns no ORIG/AP record (common for legacy BLAs and transferred applications). MISMATCH_DATE / MISMATCH_BRAND — genuine source-vs-source conflicts, surfaced for human review rather than silently overwritten.
Honest reading of the result. Rather than assert that all 1,427 rows are correct, this table shows exactly how many are machine-confirmed against a primary FDA record and how many are not. Rows that two official FDA sources disagree about are flagged, not resolved by guessing — for example FDA's 2002 NME report prints Extraneal as 12-Dec-2002 while Drugs@FDA records 20-Dec-2002. Every one of the 1,387 rows of FDA's official CDER Novel Drug Approvals Compilation (media/177921 XLSX) is matched to the master list by application number or brand+date, and every master row not on the Compilation is shown in the reverse direction. 📚 About & Methodology — How This Decision Engine Works

🎯 What This Project Is

A decision engine for FDA catalysts in publicly traded biotech and pharma: collect FDA action, verified stock-price reaction around it, and company's numeric regulatory track record, in one place, with official source link on every row. Scope deliberately limited to equities US investor can actually buy (US listings and US OTC ADRs); private applicants and non-US-only listings live on own tabs.

Goal: Determine outcome of FDA decision and likelihood, using known scientific literature and FDA decision-making expertise. Serve as decision engine for biotech investors tracking upcoming clinical trial endpoints reviewed by FDA and forward-tracking stock prices using real verified official prices.

🔁 Label Expansions — the 4,482-row extension (added 2026-09-16)

The single largest body of officially documented FDA efficacy decisions is not novel approvals — it is efficacy supplements: FDA decisions granting a new indication, new population, or new efficacy claim on an already-approved NDA/BLA. FDA approves roughly 130–600 of these per year versus ~50 novel drugs.

Why this was the right way to add 1,000+ new entries. The earlier limitation note in this repository was correct: FDA only approves ~50 novel drugs a year, so 1,000 new novel approvals beyond the existing 1,427 (1985–2026) do not exist and could only have been manufactured. Efficacy supplements are the honest alternative — every one is a real, dated FDA decision on a publicly traded company's drug, and every field can be copied verbatim from an official FDA record. This pass added 4,482 verified decisions covering all 27 years 2000–2026, of which 3,052 belong to US-investable issuers and 4,204 link FDA's own signed approval-letter PDF.

  • Source: openFDA Drugs@FDA API, filtered to submission_type=SUPPL, submission_class_code=EFFICACY, submission_status=AP. The filter is applied per submission object, not across the record — openFDA flattens nested fields, so naive querying would match applications where different submissions satisfy different clauses.
  • Collected on GitHub Actions (fetch_jobs/openfda_efficacy_supplements.json), with every request URL and payload SHA-256 written to data/raw/openfda_efficacy_supplements/manifest.json so any row can be re-derived and audited.
  • Indication text is deliberately absent. FDA publishes no structured indication field for supplements. Paraphrasing one would be exactly the kind of invention this project forbids, so each row instead links the FDA approval letter and label that state the indication in FDA's own words. Blank beats guessed.
  • Sponsor resolution is conservative. openFDA sponsor strings ("MSD SUB MERCK", "PF PRISM CV") are matched to issuers only by exact key, or by a strictly most-specific containment rule. Ambiguous cases are left UNRESOLVED (REVIEW). Issuers that are identified but have no US-listed equity are marked NO_US_TICKER — a different fact from "unresolved", and recorded as such. Historical tickers (CBST, ALXN) are downgraded to FORMERLY US-LISTED so they are never presented as tradeable.

🔍 Verification Audit — proving the "no hallucinations" claim

A source link only claims a row matches its source. scripts/crosscheck_master_vs_openfda.py re-opens the primary openFDA Drugs@FDA record for all 1,427 novel-approval rows and compares sponsor, brand and decision date field by field, writing data/verification_crosscheck.csv. Results are shown on the Verification Audit tab.

Two deliberate design choices make the audit trustworthy rather than flattering:

  • It reports what it cannot check. Rows citing no application number, or whose application openFDA has no ORIG/AP record for, are labelled as such instead of being counted as verified.
  • It never resolves a conflict by guessing. Where two official FDA sources disagree, both values and both links are recorded. Real examples surfaced by this audit: FDA's 2002 NME report prints Extraneal as 12-Dec-2002 while Drugs@FDA records 20-Dec-2002; FDA's NME compilation and 2004 report give Macugen 17-Dec-2004 while Drugs@FDA records 17-Sep-2004. These are genuine upstream discrepancies in FDA's own systems, and they are surfaced, not smoothed over.
  • Known-benign differences are classified, not hidden. FDA's published approval reports and Drugs@FDA routinely differ by 1–3 days on the same action (action date vs. submission-status date). Those are labelled DATE_DIFFERS_FROM_DRUGSFDA_1_3D rather than being counted either as clean matches or as errors.

🎯 Decision Engine — Scientific Literature & FDA Expertise

Site serves as decision engine ultimately to determine outcome of FDA decision and likelihood. Built using known scientific literature and FDA decision-making expertise:

  • Base rates: BIO / Biomedtracker / Informa, Clinical Development Success Rates 2011–2020 — NDA/BLA → approval 90.6% (n=1,453), Phase III → NDA/BLA 57.8%, Phase I → approval 7.9%, per-disease-area tables, per-modality tables
  • Accelerated approval: HHS OIG OEI-01-21-00401 (13% withdrawn), peer-reviewed accelerated-approval series (52% of 205 oncology indications converted to regular approval, 15% withdrawn, 33% ongoing; 77%/23% in 133-indication Lancet eClinicalMedicine series; 75% conversion in 57 non-oncology indications)
  • Review pathway: Priority Review = FDA judges drug potentially offers significant improvement — positive signal. Breakthrough Therapy = preliminary clinical evidence of substantial improvement — strong predictor. Accelerated Approval = surrogate endpoint, confirmatory trial risk — treated as risk factor (-0.5 weight)
  • Company track record: Repeat approvals = experienced regulatory/CMC organization. Prior CRL raises probability of review issues recurring. Repeat CRLs (Aldeyra/Reproxalap: 3 CRLs same indication) strong negative signal
  • Advisory committee: Favorable vote followed by approval in large majority; adverse vote FDA follows more often than not; convening committee signals contested benefit-risk
