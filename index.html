The scoring model

Total score (0–100) = Outcome (30) + Market validation (25) + Experience (20) + Pathway quality (15) + Pipeline progression (10) Outcome (30 pts) = 30 × clamp( (shrunk_rate − 0.75) / 0.25, 0, 1 ) where shrunk_rate = (approvals + k·p₀) / (decisions + k), k = 3, p₀ = 0.906 p₀ is the VERIFIED population base rate for a filed NDA/BLA (BIO / Biomedtracker / Informa, 2011–2020, n = 1,453) Market validation (25 pts) = 25 × [ 0.6 × share of priced approval events with a positive T+1 move + 0.4 × clamp(median T+1 move / +25%, 0, 1) ] Experience (20 pts) = 20 × clamp( log2(1 + decisions tracked) / log2(1 + 12), 0, 1 ) Pathway quality (15 pts) = 15 × [ share of approvals granted Priority Review − 0.5 × share of approvals that were Accelerated Approval ] (clamped 0…1) Pipeline progression (10) = 10 × advanced_to_next_phase / (advanced + paused/clinical-hold) — only where the pipeline scorecard is a verified deep dive; otherwise the 10 pts are redistributed pro-rata and the company is labelled lower confidence.
  • Shrinkage toward a verified external base rate, not toward zero. The Outcome component used to be the Wilson 95% lower bound of the approval rate. That punished thin evidence asymmetrically: a company with 1 approval and 0 CRLs scored ~21%, which implies a 79% failure rate that no published dataset supports — FDA approves roughly 90.6% of filed applications (BIO / Biomedtracker / Informa 2011–2020, n = 1,453 including resubmissions). The score now shrinks each company's record toward that verified prior with k = 3 pseudo-observations, so one approval scores 92.9% and a company only departs from the base rate once it has enough verified decisions to justify it.
  • A calibration band keeps the component discriminating. Because shrinkage compresses everything toward 90.6%, the shrunk rate is mapped through a 75%–100% band anchored on the published spread (BIO's per-disease-area filing-to-approval rates run from 82.5% in cardiovascular, n = 80, to 100% in allergy, n = 20; antisense is the weakest modality at 66.7%). The population base rate therefore earns 0.62 of the 30 points — an average filer, not a top score — while a sponsor with a CRL on the record falls sharply. The raw rate and the Wilson lower bound are still published next to the score as statistics.
  • Market validation and Experience were re-weighted upward (20→25 and 15→20) to pay for the Outcome change: the market term is the only component built on exchange data rather than on our own counting of FDA outcomes, and a longer verified record is the main protection against reading luck as skill.
  • Accelerated approval is treated as a risk factor (−0.5 weight) because it is granted on a surrogate endpoint that a confirmatory trial must later verify; several accelerated approvals in this dataset were later withdrawn (Ukoniq, Relyvrio, Pepaxto, Exkivity).
  • Market validation uses only verified closes from the price-snapshot table. If the market repeatedly sells a company's approvals, the score reflects that even though the FDA said yes.
  • Confidence is reported alongside every score: High = ≥8 verified decisions and a deep-dive pipeline scorecard; Moderate = ≥3 verified decisions; Low = fewer, or an FDA-decisions-only view. Scores are never presented without their confidence tier.
  • Grades: A ≥ 80, B 65–79, C 50–64, D 35–49, E < 35.

Decision Engine — modelled probability of a positive FDA action

A transparent Bayesian odds calculator. It starts from a published base rate (FDA / BIO-Informa-QLS / FDA accelerated-approval programme), then applies a small number of documented odds multipliers — review pathway, the company's own verified track record, asset characteristics, advisory-committee status — and prints the arithmetic so any reviewer can redo it. Every prior and every multiplier is listed with its source.

This is a decision-support model, not a forecast and not investment advice. Multipliers are deliberately coarse and are stated openly so they can be challenged; the base rates are citable published figures, not fitted numbers.

Stock Price Snapshots

Closing prices immediately before, on, and after each FDA decision, pulled from the Yahoo Finance chart API (/v8/finance/chart/<TICKER>?interval=1d). Each fetch's reported company name was checked against the expected issuer before the row was used, and the raw captures are stored verbatim in data/staging/ so any number can be re-checked. Non-US listings are in native currency (JPY/EUR/CHF) and noted. Rows are left blank — never estimated — where history could not be retrieved (delisted or acquired tickers, or a bar not yet published at capture time). Two series were rejected rather than recorded because the API returned data that could not be trusted as traded prices: Nuvalent (2026-07-22, degenerate zero-volume series) and Trevena/TRVN (Aug 2020 closes of 1,450–1,956 against a current price of $0.011, i.e. a large reverse-split factor applied to raw and adjusted closes alike). ESPR, YMAB and CPRX returned no data for their windows, so those cells are blank.

Complete Response Letters (Rejections)

FDA rejections and regulatory setbacks for publicly traded companies — the irregularities worth tracking, including repeat-CRL cases. C004 (Outlook Therapeutics / Lytenava) is marked RESOLVED: after a third CRL on 2025-12-31 the BLA was resubmitted and approved on 2026-07-24.

About & Methodology

What this project is

A decision engine for FDA catalysts in publicly traded biotech and pharma: collect the FDA action, the verified stock-price reaction around it, and the company's numeric regulatory track record, in one place, with an official source link on every row. Scope is deliberately limited to equities a US investor can actually buy (US listings and US OTC ADRs); private applicants and non-US-only listings live on their own tabs.

Sources

  • FDA decisions — FDA.gov Novel Drug Approvals pages, FDA's official annual New Drug Therapy Approvals reports (Appendix A = the novel-approval list, Appendix B = designations), the Internet Archive's captures of FDA pages that have since been taken offline, the official openFDA Drugs@FDA API (api.fda.gov/drug/drugsfda.json, used to resolve the applicant of record and the review priority for every application number), the official openFDA CRL transparency API (api.fda.gov/transparency/crl.json), accessdata.fda.gov approval letters and labels, and FDA Drug Trials Snapshots.
  • Stock prices — Yahoo Finance chart API (query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/<TICKER>), captured for a window around every decision date and stored verbatim in data/staging/. Each response's meta.longName and meta.fullExchangeName were checked against the expected issuer and exchange before the row was accepted.
  • Company / pipeline detail — the company's own press releases and SEC filings plus reputable biotech trade press, always cited with a direct link.
  • Base rates for the decision engine — FDA PDUFA / CDER reporting, BIO–QLS–Informa Clinical Development Success Rates 2011–2020, and FDA's accelerated-approval programme pages, each linked next to the figure.

Verification approach

Every row carries a verification_status. Rows are marked Verified only when the company / ticker / date combination was corroborated by at least one official or primary source. Anything ambiguous is flagged in the notes rather than silently resolved:

  • Applicant vs. current holder. FDA lists the current applicant of record, which changes after a licence-out or acquisition. Veppanu (NDA 219835) was approved to Arvinas/Pfizer and licensed to Rigel eleven days later; Lynavoy (NDA 220295) was approved to GSK and then licensed to Alfasigma, so the FDA record now reads "INTERCEPT". Both rows state the change.
  • Withdrawn products. Pepaxto (NDA 214383) no longer exists in the FDA application database because it was withdrawn in Oct 2021 after its confirmatory trial failed; its sponsor is marked not machine-verifiable instead of being guessed. Exkivity, Truseltiq, Ukoniq and Relyvrio are flagged as voluntarily withdrawn.
  • Data conflicts. FDA records Lynavoy's approval as 2026-03-17 while GSK announced it 2026-03-19; both dates are given. Outlook Therapeutics was approved on 2026-07-24 yet fell 24.2% and 15.1% over the next two sessions — the cause is flagged for manual review, not attributed.
  • Rejected data. One price series (Nuvalent) was discarded because the API returned a degenerate zero-volume series; the cells are blank, not filled in.
  • Blank beats guessed. No price, date, pathway or outcome anywhere in this dataset is estimated. Where a value could not be verified in the pass that produced it, the cell is empty and the reason is written into notes.

Irregularities flagged in the 2018–2020 backfill (2026-09-12)

  • 100 new verified rows — 36 from 2018, 24 from 2019 and 40 from 2020 — were added, taking the master list to 410 decisions. 44 of the new rows carry an explicit FLAGGED verification status.
  • FDA's own page has a date typo. The archived 2018 table prints Firdapse's approval as 11/28/2028. The row sits between Xospata (11/28/2018) and Vitrakvi (11/26/2018) and links the November 2018 press release, so 2018-11-28 is recorded and the typo is flagged in the row.
  • openFDA reports the CURRENT holder, which is often not the 2020 applicant. Gavreto now resolves to RIGEL PHARMS, Zokinvy to SENTYNL THERAPS INC, Nurtec ODT to PfIZER, Copiktra to SECURA, Sunosi to AXSOME MALTA, Vyleesi to COSETTE, Scenesse to CLIVUNEL INC, Vitrakvi to BAYER HEALTHCARE, Tepezza to HORIZON THERAPEUTICS IRELAND, Trodelvy to IMMUNOMEDICS INC, Tazverik to EPIZYME INC, Uplizna to VIELA BIO and Ongentys to AMNEAL. The original approval letters were not re-read in this pass, so the issuer at the decision date is left unverified rather than guessed — and for those rows no price reaction is attributed, because a price for the wrong issuer would be worse than no price.
  • Unresolvable sponsor terms. openFDA returns HONG KONG for Xenleta/lefamulin, LXO IRELAND for Barhemsys, VANCOCIN ITALIA for Mulpleta and ACACIA for Byfavo. Recorded verbatim; no company name or ticker is asserted.
  • Applications that openFDA can no longer resolve: Aemcolo, Lumoxiti, Tegsedi, Xeglyze, Pizensy, Artesunate, Gallium 68 PSMA-11, fluorodopa F 18 and gallium Ga 68 DOTATOC. The approvals themselves are verified by the FDA tables, so the rows are kept with company_name = applicant not machine-verifiable.
  • Zulresso (brexanolone, 19 March 2019) is excluded. openFDA returns no record for the brand, the generic or NDA212773, so the applicant cannot be machine-verified — the same rule already applied to Pepaxto. Excluded rather than attributed from memory.
  • Coverage gaps, stated rather than filled: rows 51–53 of FDA's 2020 table (row 51 is Orgovyx, whose table line was not readable in the archive chunk) and rows 23–1 of the 2018 table are captured but not yet sponsor-verified. 2019 is complete except Zulresso (47 of FDA's 48).
  • Ten existing rows were reclassified. Sun Pharma (NSE/BSE India; no US ADR), CSL (ASX Australia; no US ADR primary), Aurobindo, four Tokyo-only listings and Oncopeptides (Nasdaq Stockholm; no US listing) had been counted as US-LISTED (ADR) because the classifier matched the substring "ADR" inside the phrase "no US ADR". They are now NON-US LISTING ONLY, and the classifier tests non-US venues and explicit "no US equity" statements before the ADR rule.
  • Row D113 needs review. It attributes "Recordati Rare Diseases (current holder)" to Sanofi's ADR (SNY). Recordati S.p.A. is a Milan-listed issuer; the new Isturisa row is classified non-US and flags the inconsistency.

Accelerated approval is a separate question from filing success. HHS OIG (OEI-01-21-00401) found FDA and sponsors withdrew 13% of all accelerated approvals, half of them since January 2021. For oncology, 205 indications approved 1992–2024 show 52% converted to regular approval, 15% withdrawn and 33% still ongoing; a Lancet eClinicalMedicine series of 133 oncology indications found 77% converted and 23% withdrawn (median 3.81 years to withdrawal); non-oncology indications convert more often (75% of 57 indications, 1992–2018, with 10% of confirmatory trials failing on efficacy). The engine uses 52% as the conservative oncology conversion prior and 13% as the all-indication withdrawal prior.

Known limitations

  • review_pathway is populated from FDA's official annual reports and from the review_priority field of openFDA Drugs@FDA. Where neither source is unambiguous, the cell is deliberately blank.
  • FDA has taken its 2018–2020 Novel Drug Approvals web pages offline; those cohorts cite the surviving official annual-report PDFs and Internet Archive captures of the official FDA pages.
  • Approvals whose applicant was later acquired often have no retrievable price history from the chart API; those rows say so explicitly.
  • Pipeline scorecards that are not deep dives count only the FDA decisions verified in this repository and report phase progression as 0 rather than estimating it — they are labelled "Verified – FDA decisions only (partial pipeline view)" and score at lower confidence.
